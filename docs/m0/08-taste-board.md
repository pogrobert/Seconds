# Taste board: what Robert loves

**Date:** 2026-11-15. **Board:** a private review page.
**Marks:** 7 love, 30 good, 22 not for me, out of 59 real small-business sites. No notes. Stored in `tools/eval/taste/2026-11-15-board.json`.

## The 7 loved sites

| Site | What it is | What the first screen does |
|---|---|---|
| Barr Build | builder, UK | A house in a lavender meadow fills the screen. A thin serif wordmark sits in the middle, with almost no menu. |
| Castor Fleuriste | florist, Paris | A white wall, a door and one vase of flowers, in daylight. A small centred serif name, and the address in the corners. |
| Core Atelier | Pilates studio, Singapore | A warm, golden studio interior fills the screen. A large light serif headline with one word in italic, bottom left. |
| Era Ceramics | ceramicist, US | Two photos of plates side by side, red pigment against grey stone. A huge thin lowercase wordmark runs across both. |
| Librería Casa Bosques | bookshop, Mexico City | A magazine spread: the shop in daylight on one half, white space and a small product photo on the other. |
| Sóller Tennis Club | sports club, Mallorca | Mountains and a terracotta court fill the screen. Spaced capitals sit over the photo. |
| The Rechabite | bar, Perth | The exception: a black page with chunky lettering in which drawn characters replace letters. |

## What six of the seven share

1. **One beautiful photo of the real place fills the first screen.** Natural daylight, warm and rich colour (lavender, terracotta, gold, red pigment, mountains), calm composition with room to breathe.
2. **The type is quiet and refined, and it sits on the photo.** Thin serifs, or widely spaced capitals; one italic word at most. The wordmark is the headline; there is little else.
3. **The colour comes from the photo, not from the interface.** Cream, white or warm neutrals; no coloured bands or panels on the first screen.
4. **Almost no interface:** a tiny menu, one quiet button or none.

## What sets loved apart from good

The "good" pile also has photo-led, quiet sites: Löyly, Stykka, the podiatry clinic, Studio Dental. The difference is the photo itself. The loved photos are warm, sunlit and full of colour. The "good" ones are cooler, greyer or darker: the dark-and-moody sites (BXR, Bathhouse, Thomas May, Saipua) all landed in "good".

## What was "not for me"

Most of the loud, flat-colour and retro sites:
- the orange-and-yellow bakery
- the electric-blue pest control firm
- the colour-block boulangerie
- the gradient taqueria, the retro-script hotel and the terracotta yoga studio
- the colourful psychology practice

Also several stark minimal ones: the black sushi bar, the monochrome clinic, the white furniture site, the bare text menu.

## What this means for the product

- **Photography is the biggest lever, more than layout or fonts.** Our test photos (Pixabay, chosen by a search query) are ordinary. On a real site, the photos come from the owner.
- **So the library needs two things:**
  - directions that make an ordinary photo look its best: crop, a gentle warm grade, and type that sits well on it
  - at least one direction that does not depend on a great photo, for owners whose photos are weak
- **Interface colour should stay quiet.** Our coloured bands and blocks, and the Poster family's flat colour, pull against this taste. The blind test's preference for Poster was relative: it beat our weak Soft.

## Proposed directions (for Robert to confirm before any design work)

1. **Landscape:** a full-screen photo of the place, with a thin serif or spaced-capitals wordmark over it and almost no interface. *From Barr, Sóller, Castor.*
2. **Atelier:** a full-screen interior photo, with a large light serif headline (one word in italic) at the lower left. *From Core Atelier.*
3. **Spread:** a magazine spread, with the place on one half, white space and a small second photo on the other, and small type. *From Casa Bosques.*
4. **Wordmark:** a huge thin lowercase name running across two photos side by side. *From Era.*
5. **Signature:** a bold graphic identity on a dark ground, for owners who want character and have no good photos. *From The Rechabite.* This one needs drawn elements; it is the hardest to make automatic.

Each direction would get one complete homepage, designed by hand for one sample business, with better photos chosen by hand. Robert rates each one against a fixed bar: would he proudly hand it to the owner?
